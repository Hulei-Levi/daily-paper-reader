ABSTRACT 

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) enable event-driven computation through biolog-ically inspired dynamics at the neuronal scale, while Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) perform conditional computation through expert selection at the model scale. In-tegrating their strengths offers potential for flexible neural architectures. A key challenge, however, lies in designing an expert selection mechanism based on spiking activity. To address this, we introduce a spike-based k-WTA Router in-spired by competition-inhibition observed in the hippocampal CA1 region. The router incorporates lateral inhibition and refractory period to select Top-K experts according to discrete spike counts. Building on this, we present SpikeMoE, a framework that integrates neuronal-scale spiking dynamics with model-scale ex-pert selection. To address incomplete multisensory inputs in multimodal tasks, we further equip SpikeMoE with a two-stage missing-modality modeling mod-ule that combines empirical prototypes from an observed-modality pool with modality-specific learnable embeddings to construct missing-modality representa-tions. Experiments on vision, language, and multimodal benchmarks demonstrate that SpikeMoE achieves state-of-the-art performance among the SNN baselines, matches or exceeds the performance of ANN counterparts, and maintains robust-ness across diverse missing-modality conditions. These results demonstrate a favorable trade-off between performance and energy efficiency, validating the in-tegration of spiking dynamics with sparse expert computation and highlighting SpikeMoE as a promising approach to energy-efficient brain-inspired computing. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence has given rise to increasingly complex neural network models, achieving success in numerous fields. However, modern neural networks consume vast amounts of computation and memory (Hwang et al., 2024). With the continuous growth in demand for intelligent systems, developing energy-efficient computing paradigms has become a critical priority. Nature provides an encouraging blueprint for high-efficiency computing. The human brain executes complex cognitive tasks with far greater efficiency than modern AI systems, all while consuming significantly less energy (Amunts et al., 2016). This remarkable efficiency stems from two key principles: at the microscopic level, biological neurons are activated by discrete, event-driven spikes rather than continuous signals (Roy et al., 2019), enabling sparse and asynchronous computation (Yao et al., 2023; 2024); at the macroscopic level, the brain exhibits distributed processing by selectively activating distinct regions for specific tasks (O’Doherty et al., 2021), enabling robust environmental perception through precisely coordinated multisensory integration. Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) (Izhikevich, 2003; Maass, 1997; Masquelier et al., 2008), con-sidered the third generation of neural networks (Maass, 1997), simulate the dynamics of biological neurons at the microscopic level (Roy et al., 2019). The event-driven (Wei et al., 2024) charac-teristic of SNNs means that neurons fire spikes only when activated and remain silent otherwise (Akopyan et al., 2015). This mechanism increases the sparsity of synaptic computation and elimi-nates multiplication-accumulation (MAC) operations (Davies et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023b), thereby significantly improving energy efficiency. Combining SNNs with MoE is promising because they provide complementary forms of efficient computation: SNNs exploit sparse, event-driven neuronal dynamics, whereas MoE performs con-ditional computation through expert selection. Nevertheless, developing a flexible spiking MoE presents two related challenges. At the mechanism level, conventional MoE routers typically generate continuous expert scores using softmax, whose exponentiation and global normalization operations are not naturally compatible with the discrete, event-driven computation of SNNs. A central challenge is therefore to perform effective expert selection directly based on spiking activity. At the perception level, multisensory inputs in real-world scenarios are frequently incomplete (Han et al., 2024; Glasser et al., 2016; Xu et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2022). Missing modalities deprive the router and experts of important contextual cues, potentially disrupting expert selection. Without appropriately representing the missing information and exploiting the available modalities, the resulting decisions can become fragile and suboptimal (Shaik et al., 2024; Cao et al., 2023; Tsai et al., 2019; Jiang et al., 2023; Peng et al., 2022). To address these challenges, we propose SpikeMoE, a unified and flexible framework that integrates the strengths of SNNs and MoE. To resolve the internal bottleneck, we draw inspiration from hippocampal CA1 calcium imaging to propose k-WTA Router which is based on competition-inhibition mechanism (Topolnik & Tamboli, 2022). This approach achieves dynamic expert selection via sparse spike activation, effectively mitigating the issue of expert collapse. To overcome external perception challenges and improve robustness against incomplete inputs, we introduce a two-stage missing-modality modeling strategy. This enables the model to flexibly handle arbitrary combinations of modalities. The main contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce a k-WTA Router based on competition-inhibition mechanism. By incorporat-ing lateral inhibition and refractory dynamics, the router performs Top-K expert selection according to spike counts and mitigates repeated within-input activation of a single expert. • We develop a two-stage missing-modality modeling strategy that combines empirical infor-mation from an modality pool with modality-specific learnable embeddings, which enables the robust modeling of arbitrary modality combinations. • By integrating the k-WTA Router with the missing-modality modaling strategy, we present SpikeMoE, a unified and flexible framework that connects neuronal-scale spiking dynamics with modal-scale sparse expert activation. • Extensive evaluations show that SpikeMoE achieves performance comparable to or better than its ANN counterparts and establishes state-of-the-art results among the evaluated SNN baselines. Moreover, it remains robust under diverse missing-modality conditions while offering a favorable performance–energy efficiency trade-off. 2Preprint 

2 RELATED WORKS 

Multimodal learning in SNNs. With recent advances in neuromorphic computing, exploration of multimodal SNNs has gained momentum. We summarize main studies according to different categories. a. Audiovisual classification. In (Liu et al., 2025), the introduction of MLIF neurons synchronizes and coordinates the spike activation of audiovisual signals within a single neuron; Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2022) proposed an event-based multimodal SNN comprising visual and auditory unimodal subnetworks and an attention-based cross-modal subnetwork for fusion. A spiking multimodal transformer was also proposed for multimodal audiovisual classification (Guo et al., 2023). b. Emotion Recognition. Tan et al. (Tan et al., 2020) employed an evolutionary architecture of multimodal SNNs based on the NeuCube framework; the Sliding Parallel Spiking Conv-ViT (Chen et al., 2025) integrated facial expressions, speech, and text, utilizing aligned heatmap features and multiscale wavelet transforms for precise feature extraction. 

Multimodal learning in missing modality scenario. In many tasks, considering interactions among multimodal data can provide comprehensive understanding for downstream applications. MISTS (Zhang et al., 2022) employs a multi-time attention mechanism to encode irregular time series and utilizes multimodal cross-attention to fuse different modalities. In real-world scenarios, missing modalities pose a critical challenge. (Du et al., 2018; Shang et al., 2017) introduce generative adver-sarial networks to estimate missing modalities through semi-supervised learning; ShaSpec (Wang et al., 2023a) employs multiple loss functions to learn the proportion and specific representations of different modalities. However, the reconstruction networks in ShaSpec require multiple additional auxiliary loss functions, consuming substantial computational resources during optimization. Further-more, recent studies like FuseMoE (Han et al., 2024) respect modal absence and aim to assign each combination of multimodal data to distinct experts.

4 METHODS 

4.1 K-WTA R OUTER IN SPIKE MOEBuilding upon the original Transformer architecture, MoE introduces a substantial number of parame-ters and achieves conditional computation by calculating the routing probability via softmax function. However, SNNs must avoid multiplication, floating-point routing experts and softmax involving exponentiation and division do not adhere to the computational principles of SNNs. Therefore, it is necessary to design SNN-friendly gated routing mechanisms. 

Competition-inhibition mechanism. Neuroscientists have observed lateral inhibition in calcium imaging results from the hippocampal CA1 region (Figure 2(a)) (Topolnik & Tamboli, 2022). It plays a vital role in information encoding and processing by enhancing neurons’ specific responses to stimuli and improving spatial resolution in information processing (Topolnik & Tamboli, 2022; Jeong & Singer, 2022). It serves as a crucial mechanism for selective learning. Inspired by this phenomenon, we propose a novel spiking gating network based on a competition-inhibition mechanism, named the k-WTA (Winner-Take-All) Router. 6 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we propose SpikeMoE, a unified framework that integrates Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) with the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture. To address the inherent conflict between traditional softmax gating and SNN dynamics, we introduce a brain-inspired spiking gating network based on a competition-refractory-inhibition mechanism, which achieves dynamic expert selection through sparse spiking activation. Furthermore, to enhance the model’s adaptability in complex scenarios, we propose a two-stage missing-modality modeling strategy. Extensive evaluations across vision, language, and multimodal datasets demonstrate SpikeMoE’s robust performance. Future work will focus on applying SpikeMoE to a broader range of tasks, scaling the framework to larger architectures, and exploring its deployment on neuromorphic hardware. AI USE STATEMENT

RE×E is the learnable inhibition weight matrix, M ∈ RE×E is the inhibition mask matrix, and γ is the inhibition strength coefficient. This mechanism adjusts the membrane potential but does not alter the form of the input current I[t] calculation. • As shown in equation 9, the refractory period mechanism affects membrane potential reset and spike emission. Here, R[t] ∈ RB×E is the refractory period counter. When a neuron spikes, the membrane potential is reset to V [t] ← 0 and R[t] ← 2, but this does not involve the calculation of the input current. Therefore, the input current Ii[t] is always composed of the weighted sum of the spike outputs from the presynaptic neurons, consistent with Definition 1. In summary, the routing and expert-selection processes in k-WTA Router exhibit spike-driven characteristics. A.2 EXPERIMENT DETAILS

ANN Baselines. ShaSpec (Wang et al., 2023a) employs multiple loss functions to learn both modality-shared and modality-specific information, and utilizes the shared information to estimate missing modalities. TF (Zadeh et al., 2017) extracts modality interactions through multimodal sub-networks and a tensor fusion layer. mmFormer (Zhang et al., 2022), a typical multimodal Transformer, features multi-head self-attention mechanisms. FuseMoE (Han et al., 2024) respects missing modalities and jointly learns from both single and missing modality combinations of data inputs. In experiments, FuseMoE utilizes a softmax gate. 

SNN Baselines. Spikformer (Zhou et al., 2022) pioneers the integration of Transformer architectures into SNNs by proposing Spiking Self-Attention. It significantly enhances the representation capacity of SNNs for visual feature extraction while maintaining biological interpretability. Spike-driven Transformer (Yao et al., 2023) introduces a purely event-driven attention paradigm. It effectively replaces all dense matrix multiplications in vanilla Transformers with sparse addition operations based on spike masks. Spikingformer (Zhou et al., 2023) further improves SNN-based Transformers by optimizing spike-driven residual learning and feature mapping mechanisms. SpikeLM (Xing et al., 2024) is an advanced spiking language model specifically designed for natural language processing (NLP) tasks. By adapting spike representations for text sequences, it demonstrates the effectiveness and scalability of SNNs on standard language understanding benchmarks such as GLUE. (Zhou et al., 2022) . WeightAttention (Liu et al., 2022) dynamically assigns weights to measure the importance of visual and auditory modalities, thereby effectively integrating information from these two modalities. 

SCA (Guo et al., 2023) integrates image and audio sub-modules and combines relative positional bias (RPB) to enhance the ability to capture information from different positions. S-CMRL (He et al., 2025) employs spatiotemporal spike attention to extract complementary features across modalities and combines cross-modal residual learning to enhance feature integration. Additionally, S-CMRL introduces a semantic alignment optimization mechanism to align cross-modal features within a shared semantic space, thereby improving their consistency and robustness.
