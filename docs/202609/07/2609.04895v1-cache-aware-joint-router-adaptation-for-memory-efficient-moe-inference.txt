ABSTRACT 

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models activate only a small subset of experts per to-ken, but the full expert set often exceeds GPU memory, causing repeated weight transfers during decoding. We formulate expert-cache management as a model-side algorithmic problem and propose a cache-aware post-training framework that jointly adapts the MoE backbone and lightweight auxiliary cache routers while preserving the native Top-K expert-selection rule at inference. Its update-only mode, Temporal Router , predicts same-layer reuse and retains experts for fu-ture tokens without proactive loading. The full Spatio-Temporal Router adds a 

Spatio Router that uses the causal predecessor’s hidden state to refine the tem-poral cache before target-layer access. We evaluate both modes on Qwen3 and GPT-OSS across GSM8K, MATH, and CommonsenseQA. Temporal Router con-sistently improves cache hit rate and reduces expert-weight traffic over matched LM-only baselines. On Qwen3, Spatio-Temporal Router achieves the best load-adjusted efficiency across three tasks, improving adjusted hit rate by 1.15–18.03 points and reducing traffic by 4.6–53.3% relative to the strongest evaluated prefetching baseline; results on GPT-OSS are competitive but task-dependent. An auxiliary-only ablation preserves baseline accuracy but yields modest cache gains, whereas joint post-training produces larger improvements. Sensitivity analyses show that cache capacity controls transfer demand, while the refinement budget governs the trade-off between pre-access coverage and proactive traffic. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) is a central architecture for improving the capacity–computation trade-off of Transformer models. By routing each token to a small expert subset, an MoE layer increases total capacity without proportional per-token computation. Yet computational sparsity does not ensure efficient serving: growing expert sets may exceed an inference worker’s GPU memory, forcing decode-time weight transfers from slower memory. This deployment gap has motivated expert offloading, caching, and prefetching. MoE-Infinity re-duces offloading overhead through activation-aware caching and request-level tracing Xue et al. (2024). ProMoE predicts future expert use from intermediate hidden states and proactively fetches experts Song et al. (2024), while FineMoE uses fine-grained access patterns and prompt-level sig-nals for expert placement and replacement Yu et al. (2026a). Classical LRU, LFU, and LRFU poli-cies provide training-free alternatives Mattson et al. (1970); Robinson & Devarakonda (1990); Lee et al. (2001). More recent MoE-specific methods include Least-Stale eviction in SpecMD Hoang et al. (2026) and the option-based expert-set persistence of Temporally Extended MoE Shen & Hen-derson (2026). Closest to our setting, STEP introduces adaptive spatio-temporal expert prefetch-ing Liu et al. (2026), whereas ST-MoE combines profiled cross-token and cross-layer correlations with lightweight runtime prediction and reconfigurable hardware Zhao et al. (2026).

First, heuristic or replay-based cache policies cannot directly opti-mize which resident experts remain useful at later decoding steps. Second, recent spatio-temporal prefetchers mainly treat locality as a runtime prediction and scheduling problem. We instead learn cache priorities during post-training, jointly adapting native routing behavior and auxiliary cache routers under a cache-aware objective while leaving transfer scheduling and hardware realization outside the algorithm. We introduce a two-stage cache-management framework. After each layer access, the Temporal Router determines which experts remain cached for later tokens at the same layer. Before a target access, the optional Spatio Router uses the causal predecessor’s hidden state to refine the cache. The native MoE router selects the executed experts; the auxiliary routers manage residency. The update-only mode uses only Temporal Router and performs no proactive loading, whereas the full 

Spatio-Temporal Router adds bounded Spatio Router refinement to the temporal carry-over cache. We evaluate both modes on Qwen3 and GPT-OSS using GSM8K, MATH, and CommonsenseQA. Temporal Router consistently raises hit rate and reduces traffic relative to classical replacement configurations. Among prefetching methods, Spatio-Temporal Router achieves the highest adjusted hit rate and lowest Load on all Qwen3 tasks, with competitive GPT-OSS results and far fewer added inference-time parameters than ProMoE. Auxiliary-only training yields limited cache gains, whereas joint post-training is substantially more effective. Cache-capacity and refinement-budget sweeps show that larger caches reduce transfer demand, while aggressive refinement trades proactive traffic for higher raw hit rate. The main contributions are as follows: • We formulate expert-cache management as cache-aware model post-training that jointly adapts native routing behavior and lightweight in-model cache routers without changing the inference-time Top-K rule. • We instantiate a stackable two-stage algorithm: Temporal Router is an independently deployable update-only policy with no proactive traffic, and Spatio-Temporal Router adds bounded Spatio Router refinement to the temporally carried cache before expert access. • We compare against representative replacement and prefetching baselines on two sparse MoE backbones and three reasoning benchmarks, and analyze adaptation scope, router composition, cache capacity, refinement budget, and cache-loss weight with proactive-load-aware metrics. 

2 PROBLEM FORMULATION 

We consider autoregressive inference with a Transformer-based MoE language model under limited GPU memory. The model contains L MoE layers and N routed experts per layer. At decoding step 

t and MoE layer l, the hidden state is ht,l . The native router produces an expert distribution and selects the active experts: 

pRt,l = softmax( W Rl ht,l ) ∈ RN , St,l = TopK( pRt,l ),

where K = |St,l | is the number of activated experts. Our methods preserve this native selected set; they do not replace it with decisions from an auxiliary router. Each MoE layer maintains a bounded expert cache. After initialization, the cache is kept at capacity: 

Ct,l ⊆ { 1, . . . , N }, |C t,l | = B, 

where Ct,l is the set of resident experts available before expert access and B is the cache capacity. We assume B ≥ K, as in our experiments. Any expert in St,l \ C t,l must be demand-loaded before the corresponding expert computation can proceed. The two variants are deployment configurations of one cache-aware post-training framework. Main runs jointly optimize all backbone and auxiliary-router parameters under the language-modeling and cache objectives. Native-router distributions in the cache loss remain differentiable, allowing the objective to reshape native routing behavior as well as auxiliary priorities. The update-only mode uses LT ; the full mode trains Temporal Router and Spatio Router together with LST , without a separate Temporal Router checkpoint or an additional LT term. The LM-only reference omits auxiliary routers and optimizes LLM alone, equivalently sw = 0 . Future or target native-router distributions provide teacher-forced supervision only during post-training; inference uses causal hidden states and stored router outputs. The auxiliary-only ablation freezes the LM-only post-trained backbone, including its native routers, and trains only W T , or W T and W S . Temporal-only is the standard update-only mode. Spatio-only removes Temporal Router, trains with sSt,l = pRt−1,l + pSρ(t,l ), and performs only pre-access refinement at inference. 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Datasets. We evaluate on three reasoning benchmarks. GSM8K contains 7,473 training and 1,319 test grade-school mathematics problems requiring multi-step arithmetic reasoning Cobbe et al. (2021). MATH contains 7,500 training and 5,000 test competition-level problems spanning alge-bra, geometry, number theory, counting and probability, and related topics Hendrycks et al. (2021). CommonsenseQA is a ConceptNet-derived multiple-choice benchmark with 9,741 training, 1,221 validation, and 1,140 hidden-test questions; because the test labels are not public, we report valida-tion accuracy Talmor et al. (2019). 

Backbone models. We use two open-weight sparse MoE backbones. Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 contains 48 layers, approximately 30.5B total parameters and 3.3B activated parameters, 128 routed experts per MoE layer, and top-8 routing Team (2025). GPT-OSS-20B contains approxi-mately 21B total parameters and 3.6B activated parameters, 24 layers, 32 local experts per MoE layer, and top-4 routing Agarwal et al. (2025). Both backbones use the same post-training and evaluation pipeline; backbone-specific cache capacities are specified below. 

Baselines. For cache update, we compare with LRU and LFU Mattson et al. (1970); Robinson & Devarakonda (1990) and the recency–frequency policy LRFU Lee et al. (2001). Prefetching base-lines are Least-Stale from SpecMD Hoang et al. (2026), ProMoE Song et al. (2024), FineMoE Yu et al. (2026a), and Temporally Extended MoE Shen & Henderson (2026). LRU, LFU, and LRFU run on the matched LM-only post-trained backbone for each backbone–dataset pair ( L = LLM , or 

sw = 0 , with no auxiliary routers); learned baselines follow their prescribed adaptation. Table 1 therefore compares complete inference configurations rather than cache policies on an identical routing trace, since our configurations deliberately adapt routing behavior. We apply one cache-accounting protocol to all methods and charge every proactive insertion. STEP and ST-MoE are 6discussed qualitatively because their integrated runtime or hardware evaluations are not directly comparable to our scheduler-independent traffic analysis. 

Implementation details. All experiments run on an eight-accelerator node with 140 GB of device memory per accelerator. In the main experiments, Qwen3 uses a learning rate of 1 × 10 −5, cache size 20, and four post-training epochs; GPT-OSS uses 5 × 10 −6, cache size 8, and four epochs. All backbone and auxiliary-router parameters remain trainable. The LM-only reference uses the same data, optimizer, and schedule but omits auxiliary routers and optimizes LLM alone ( sw = 0 ). We use fused AdamW, cosine decay, 3% warmup, weight decay 0.1, gradient clipping at 1.0, per-device batch size 1, and eight gradient-accumulation steps. Training uses bfloat16, TF32, and gradient checkpointing, without LoRA or weight quantization. For the main comparison and the adapta-tion/router ablations, every configuration is run once under each seed in the same fixed five-seed set; Tables 1 and 2 report the arithmetic mean. We add no cache-specific load-balancing loss. Maxi-mum sequence lengths are 512 for GSM8K and CommonsenseQA and 2048 for MATH. We train on complete training splits for fixed epochs without validation-based checkpoint selection. Evaluation uses each backbone’s chat template and task-specific system instruction, greedy decoding with the KV cache, and limits of 512, 1,024, and 64 new tokens for GSM8K, MATH, and CommonsenseQA; generation stops at EOS or the task budget. LRFU uses λ = 0 .5. SpecMD/Least-Stale uses lookahead 3 and a prefetch budget equal to cache capacity. ProMoE uses training-split traces with lookahead 3. FineMoE uses a 1K-entry expert-map store built from training traces, lookahead 3, a cache-sized prefetch budget, 64 candidates, and retrieval top-k values of 8 for Qwen3 and 4 for GPT-OSS. Temporally Extended MoE uses a cache-sized option set, 128-dimensional controller and set embeddings, copy-router initialization, and its original losses. Each Temporal Router copies the native router at the same layer, W Tl ← W Rl . The Spatio Router at source layer l predicts the next MoE position and copies that target layer’s native router:

Metrics. We report task accuracy, hard cache hit rate, load-adjusted hit rate, and expert-weight traffic. Accuracy uses exact match for GSM8K and CommonsenseQA and symbolic or numeric matching for MATH. Cache behavior is evaluated with a trace-driven simulator synchronized with decode-time routing; each demand miss or proactive insertion is charged as one full expert transfer. We discuss update-only and prefetching methods separately: the former incur no proactive traffic, whereas the latter may spend proactive loads to improve pre-access coverage. 

Update-only cache retention. Temporal Router consistently outperforms the LM-only replace-ment configurations. On Qwen3, it gives the highest hit rate and lowest Load on all three tasks. Relative to the strongest classical hit-rate baseline, the gains are 10.46, 10.70, and 33.34 points on GSM8K, MATH, and CommonsenseQA, while Load falls from 1353 to 974, 1294 to 906, and 1512 to 304 MB/token. On GPT-OSS, it also leads all update-only methods across the three tasks while adding 2.2M inference-time parameters. 

Prefetch-enabled cache refinement. Load is the primary traffic metric, while adjusted hit rate provides a normalized view that also penalizes proactive transfers. 5 ANALYSIS AND DISCUSSION 

Effect of adaptation scope. Table 2 compares cache-aware training against the matched LM-only reference. At sw = 0 .1, auxiliary-only training preserves baseline accuracy because the backbone and executed Top-K routing remain fixed, but the cache gains are modest: Temporal Router reaches 63.01% hard hit and 1340.71 MB/token, while the full mode reaches 67.44% hard hit and 62.53% adjusted hit with 1747.59 MB/token. Joint post-training is substantially stronger. Temporal Router reaches 73.13% hard hit and 973.86 MB/token with unchanged accuracy; the full mode reaches 90.62% hard hit and 69.03% adjusted hit with a 2.04-point accuracy reduction. These results show the value of cache-aware joint adaptation: auxiliary routers alone yield modest gains, whereas adapting them with the backbone produces a much better cache operating point. The LM-only and auxiliary-only controls separate ordinary language-model post-training from cache-aware adaptation, but do not attribute the gain to one parameter subset. 

Effect of router composition. Temporal-only is the standard Temporal Router at sw = 0 .1, re-peated for direct comparison rather than trained separately. Spatio-only removes pT , trains with 

sSt,l = pRt−1,l + pSρ(t,l ), and disables learned post-access retention. Its hard hit rate exceeds the full mode at the same weight (93.04% versus 90.62%), showing better immediate pre-access coverage in this setting. However, without temporal carry-over updates, every learned replacement occurs during prefetching. Spatio-only therefore has lower adjusted hit (66.94% versus 69.03%) and much higher Load (1665.51 versus 1474.00 MB/token), so its overall traffic efficiency is worse despite the higher raw hit. 

Traffic accounting under proactive refinement. At sw = 0 .1, the auxiliary-only full mode, Spatio-only mode, and jointly adapted full mode increase Load over MoE/LRU by 341.09, 259.01, and 67.50 MB/token despite higher hard hit rates. At fixed B, increasing R monotonically improves raw hit but can over-prefetch. The main B = 20 , R = 15 setting favors pre-access coverage, whereas smaller budgets provide lower-traffic deployment points. Raw hit should therefore be interpreted with proactive-load-aware metrics. 

Effect of the cache-loss weight. We next vary sw for Qwen3 on GSM8K. This is a post hoc sensitivity analysis; the backbone-specific values in Table 1 were fixed before downstream-task evaluation and shared across datasets. For Temporal Router, increasing sw raises hard hit rate from 73.13% to 88.44% and lowers Load from 973.86 to 418.89 MB/token. Relative to MoE/LRU, this corresponds to gains of 11.94–27.25 points and traffic reductions of 432.64–987.61 MB/token. The gains come with increasing accuracy loss: sw = 0 .1 preserves baseline accuracy, sw = 0 .3 loses 0.45 points, and sw = 2 .0 loses 10.84 points. For Spatio-Temporal Router, stronger supervision raises hard hit rate from 90.62% to 97.41% and adjusted hit rate from 69.03% to 79.67%. Reduced demand traffic outweighs proactive insertions from sw = 0 .3 onward: relative to MoE/LRU, sw = 0 .1 increases Load by 67.50 MB/token, whereas sw = 0 .3 and sw = 2 .0 reduce it by 177.46 and 505.59 MB/token. Accuracy drops by 1.97–2.04 points at sw = 0 .1 and sw = 0 .3, and by 8.94 points at sw = 2 .0. Overall, sw controls the quality–traffic trade-off: moderate values capture substantial cache locality, whereas large values over-optimize transfer efficiency at the expense of task quality.

These observations clarify the quality–traffic trade-off: moderate supervision improves residency while retaining routing diversity, whereas excessive supervision concentrates traffic and correlates with lower task accuracy. 11 6 RELATED WORK 

Efficient LLM and MoE serving. Large-model serving has motivated work on mem-ory management, offloading, and parallel execution. FlexGen Sheng et al. (2023) and vLLM/PagedAttention Kwon et al. (2023) reduce memory pressure through tensor placement, KV-cache management, and batching. MoE systems additionally manage large, sparse, dynamically selected expert weights. DeepSpeed-MoE Rajbhandari et al. (2022) and Tutel Hwang et al. (2023) optimize parallel execution, while MoE-Infinity Xue et al. (2024) targets offloading through trac-ing, caching, and prefetching. These systems optimize the serving substrate; our method learns model-side cache priorities without prescribing scheduling or hardware realization. 

Expert caching and prefetching. Expert caching builds on classical replacement algorithms. LRU and LFU use recency and frequency, while LRFU interpolates between them with a decayed score Mattson et al. (1970); Robinson & Devarakonda (1990); Lee et al. (2001). These policies are training-free but cannot learn task- or model-specific reuse. MoE-specific methods use richer sig-nals: Least-Stale relies on stale-access information Hoang et al. (2026), ProMoE trains an activation-based predictor Song et al. (2024), FineMoE retrieves expert maps Yu et al. (2026a), and Temporally Extended MoE learns whether to retain or switch a layer-wise expert set Shen & Henderson (2026). Related systems also study serverless placement and speculative scheduling for distributed MoE inference Yu et al. (2026b); Li et al. (2025). 

Spatio-temporal expert prefetching. STEP proposes adaptive spatio-temporal expert prefetch-ing Liu et al. (2026), while ST-MoE combines profiled cross-layer and consecutive-token correla-tions with table-based prediction and reconfigurable hardware Zhao et al. (2026). Both emphasize inference-time prediction or system support. Our method instead learns in-model cache priorities through post-training, preserves the native Top-K rule, and supports both update-only retention and bounded pre-access refinement. Because their reported latency reflects integrated runtime or hardware pipelines, we compare designs qualitatively rather than against our scheduler-independent traffic metric. 

7 CONCLUSION 

We presented a cache-aware post-training framework for expert-cache management in memory-constrained MoE inference. Temporal Router performs update-only retention, while Spatio-Temporal Router adds bounded pre-access refinement; both preserve the native inference-time Top-

K rule. Across two backbones and three benchmarks, the proposed configurations improve cache coverage and reduce decode-stage traffic with at most 0.083% added inference-time parameters. Auxiliary-only training yields modest gains, whereas joint post-training is substantially stronger. Spatio-only refinement raises raw hit but incurs more proactive traffic without temporal updates. Finally, the cache and weight sweeps show that B, R, and sw control memory, pre-access coverage, traffic, and quality. 

LIMITATIONS 

This work studies algorithmic cache management, not an end-to-end serving stack. Load/token measures simulated decode-stage traffic; realized latency and throughput also depend on scheduling, transfer–computation overlap, bandwidth, and batching. Because prefill traffic is excluded but its cache initializes decoding, our metrics characterize decode-stage rather than request-level efficiency and do not imply end-to-end speedup. Main runs update the full backbone, so the method has small inference-time model-size overhead but requires full-model post-training. The auxiliary-only ablation shows that the new routers alone are insufficient, but does not isolate native-router adaptation from other backbone changes. The full benchmark suite uses one main cache capacity per backbone; cache-capacity sensitivity is limited to Qwen3/GSM8K. Tables 1 and 2 average the same five fixed seeds, whereas Table 3 replays each Spatio-Temporal checkpoint across three inference-time R values. 12 We focus on academic reasoning benchmarks and pure MoE models. Broader workloads, memory budgets, pretraining settings, and hybrid dense–sparse architectures remain future work. Cache-aware adaptation also concentrates routing, which may improve locality but impair expert-parallel load balance. Finally, the baselines were developed under different task definitions and system assumptions. We reproduce their algorithms under a unified cache setting and align proactive-load accounting, but relative performance may change under other memory hierarchies or runtime policies.
